## Sources consulted this session

- **S1.** Chandar S. Sundaram, "A Paper Tiger: The Indian National Army in Battle,
  1944-1945", *War & Society* 13:1 (May 1995), pp. 35-59. Read in full from a scan:
  <https://static1.squarespace.com/static/57205b34f85082c489b5c332/t/59670df586e6c013a5c29e1e/1499926059264/Paper+Tiger-+The+Indian+National+Army+in+Battle%2C+1944-1945.pdf>
  Its footnotes rest mainly on British intelligence summaries (SEAC and India Command
  WIS/WSIS), Shah Nawaz Khan's memoir, Toye, Allen, Kirby, and interviews with Sahgal,
  Dhillon and Mohan Singh. **Bias to keep in mind:** the title states its verdict and
  much of the evidence is the British intelligence view of the INA.
- **S2.** battleofimphal.com, "Imphal and INA" (site credited to Hemant Singh Katoch):
  <https://battleofimphal.com/the-battle/imphal-and-ina>. Read through a summarising
  tool, not word by word.
- **S3.** Wikipedia, "Battles and operations of the Indian National Army":
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_and_operations_of_the_Indian_National_Army>.
  Read through a summarising tool. Its footnotes cite Fay 1993 by page. **Fay has not
  been seen.** Page numbers attributed to Fay below are Wikipedia's, not ours.
- **S4.** Web search summaries (news sites, Wikipedia mirrors). Lowest confidence.

## 4. Claude's recollections that were not confirmed

These came from memory during this session. None may be used.

1. An officer of the Gandhi Brigade named Garewal deserted to the British around the
   Palel raid. A targeted search found nothing.
2. Claude recalled "Gwalior Lancers" and "L.S. Misra". S1 prints "5th Gwalior Rifles"
   and "L.N. Misra". Recorded as a worked example of why memory is not a source.
3. A British-pattern section was a Bren (gun) group plus a rifle group, about 10 men,
   drilled to cover with the gun and flank with the rifles. Web summaries support this
   for the British Army in 1944. Nothing found for the Indian Army in 1944 or the INA.
4. INA sections carried a light machine gun. Nothing found. Evidence against: Arakan
   raiding parties had rifles, pistols and a few grenades (S1 p. 43); the Palel force
   had no machine guns (S3, second-hand).
5. The INA used Hindustani written in Roman script. Supported only by a blog and a
   reported 1944 INA medical manual (S4).
6. INA rank titles for section and platoon leaders. Nothing found.
7. Japanese ammunition could not feed the INA's .303 weapons. This is reasoning from
   general weapons knowledge, not a sourced statement about INA supply.

## 5. Open questions that block mission design

1. Strength, leader's rank and weapons of a section in an INA guerrilla regiment in
   May 1944.
2. What, if anything, such a section had for suppressive fire. Pillar 1 depends on it.
3. Which battalions of 23rd Indian Division manned the patrols and road-blocks that
   Gandhi Brigade raided from mid-May to late June 1944.
4. How appeals to Indian troops were actually made on the Palel front.
5. Monsoon onset and day-to-day weather in the hills south of Palel, May to June 1944.
6. Ground around Khanjol and Mitlong Khunou: a 1944 map is needed before any map is
   drawn, along with that map's copyright status.
7. Which villages lay in the Gandhi Brigade's area, who lived there, and what happened
   to them. Section 6 of the brief requires this and nothing was found.

## 6. What this suggests for mission 1 (Claude's inference, not a decision)

In S1, the setting where the design pillars line up best is the Gandhi Brigade around
Mitlong Khunou from mid-May to late June 1944 (S1 pp. 48-49).

- Documented there: small raids on patrols and road-blocks, acute shortages, sickness,
  Indian troops opposite who did not come over, and desertion from the brigade itself.
- True by date only: the monsoon. S1 does not describe the weather at Mitlong Khunou.
- Documented nowhere found so far: how a section actually fought, which is what
  Pillar 1 (suppress, then move) is about. See open questions 1 and 2.

S1 documents the pattern, not any single raid, so a mission there would be tagged
COMPOSITE. This rests on one source and needs corroboration before it is adopted.

## 8. Books needed for page-level citations

  most of the Kaladan, Chin Hills and Palel detail.

  Prem Kumar Sahgal's point of view.

- Louis Allen, *Burma: The Longest War, 1941-1945* (London, 1984).
- K.K. Ghosh, *The Indian National Army* (Meerut, 1969).

- Published work on the Imphal battlefields by Hemant Singh Katoch, the author behind S2.
